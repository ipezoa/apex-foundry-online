Impact damage ALL CARS
1% DAMAGE = 0.5% PERFORMANCE LOSS

Impact severity grows nonlinearly with closing speed. Front: subtract 5% for the initiating driver or 10% for the receiving driver. Sides and rear: subtract 10% or 20%, respectively. Only severity above the threshold becomes damage. Even light contact can deflect the cars, but does not inflict damage. Progressive body scratches and deformation marks appear on the struck sections. 1% effective damage reduces performance by 0.5%; Reset does not repair it.

Make the track yours.

Select a section in Track Lab or the blueprint. Set straight lengths, corner direction, angle and radius, width, average incline and camber. Elevation change and incline are linked. Rescale changes the overall layout length, not road width.

The editor smoothly connects the last section to the first when needed. Inspect amber connectors in Blueprint. Select Accept connector and continue to use the generated closing section. Folded bends and road-level crossings are advisory: you can accept the layout and race as it is, but handling, collisions and AI progress may be unreliable in those sections. Approval applies to the current layout only; geometry or direction changes require a new confirmation. Elevated crossings remain supported.

Choose Street Sedan (216 km/h), Rally Special (280 km/h) or Formula One (380 km/h) in Car Selection. Everyone uses the selected class. Settings modifies each class’s base tune; AI performance percentages explicitly scale rival stats. Hard defaults to 120%, Medium to 100%, Easy to 90%. Rally has no drifting or boost. Track limits use all four tyre contact patches: after more than one second outside, all cars are capped at 80 km/h until re-entry plus two on-track seconds (editable in Race Setup). Any all-four-out excursion invalidates a qualifying lap immediately. Runoff width and surface are configurable per side and section; barriers are at the outer edge. Bank angle affects grip; gradients affect acceleration. Sharp bends still require braking. This is an arcade driving model, not a tire or suspension simulator.

Tracks save in this browser. Export JSON to keep a portable copy. No login or external analytics. Qualifying telemetry is recorded locally and can be exported; it is never uploaded. APEX FOUNDRY / ENGINEERING

Make it feel right.

Set each class’s base tune. The player uses 100% of these values; each rival’s performance percentage is applied afterward. Changes invalidate qualifying references for the affected car.

Tuning is locked during an active race. Return to the editor to change vehicle physics.

Top speed is a limiter, not a promise: acceleration, drag, hills and braking still determine attainable speed. Very high steering angles are safety-limited below 90°. Wheel positions and collision dimensions are unchanged.

25 circuit-specific scenic themes, built from original low-poly geometry and procedural skies. These are reference-inspired illustrations, not AI-generated images or surveyed replicas.

Boards show centerline distance to an automatically detected turn-in, not a guaranteed braking point or an official surveyed corner. Markers reverse with race direction. Scenery does not alter handling. Tecpro-style blocks are visual; the collision boundary is rigid.

Keep the car close at any speed. Speed pullback is an explicit extra distance, not a follow-lag effect. Set it to zero for a fixed chase distance.

High increases pixel sampling and GPU cost. Rounded tyres and body surfaces use higher-detail meshes and smoothed normals at every setting for your car. Distant opponents retain simpler geometry.

Layered engine firing, intake, turbo spool and lift-off, exhaust pops, brake squeal, tyre scrub, wheelspin and impact sounds. Every effect follows the current driving inputs; the sound model does not add drifting.
